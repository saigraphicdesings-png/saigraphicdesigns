By Sai Graphic Designs · Business planning guide

This guide uses two illustrative project briefs to show what to look for. They are planning examples, not claims about completed clients or a ranking of district designers.

Project example: A B2B supplier’s sales introduction

A B2B sales sheet should help a new buyer understand the company’s offer and take the next step. Ask for a clear headline, product or service grouping and an enquiry section that remains readable in an emailed PDF.

What to check in the proof

Use approved company details and separate technical facts from broad marketing language. Check whether the document will be printed, emailed or viewed on a phone before choosing its format.

A different brief: A business launch’s sign and stationery set

A sign, business card and invoice should share a recognizable identity. Specify the sign size and viewing distance separately from the small-format stationery requirements.

Build a useful brief for your Tiruvallur project

Start with the audience and the action you want the design to support. Then list each deliverable: size, number of pages or variants, language versions and intended use. Supply approved text and the clearest available logo or product images.

  • Language: Tell us whether you need Tamil, English or separate versions. Have a fluent reviewer approve spelling, names and technical wording.
  • Production: Send your printer’s template or dieline when relevant. Ask for a realistic size proof before approving artwork.
  • Budget: Compare the same scope, revision rounds and file formats across quotes. Additional items and a new design direction should be agreed separately.
  • Handover: Identify the editing software your team uses and the export formats your supplier needs. Editable-file delivery depends on the agreed package.

Questions from businesses planning work in Tiruvallur

Can the same logo file be used for a sign and an invoice?

A scalable master can support both, but each application needs suitable exports and layout checks. Confirm the sign maker’s production requirements before handover.
