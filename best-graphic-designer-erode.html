This guide uses two illustrative project briefs to show what to look for. They are planning examples, not claims about completed clients or a ranking of district designers.

Project example: A fabric collection’s lookbook

A fabric seller’s lookbook should make product codes, color names and available options easy to locate. Compare designers using multi-page samples, and ask how they keep spacing and product information consistent across a collection.

What to check in the proof

Use approved photographs and explain how color references will be checked. Screen previews alone should not be treated as an exact guarantee of printed fabric colors.

A different brief: A wholesale business’s order stationery

An order sheet, quotation and business card should share one identity while giving enough room for practical information. Test a sample with realistic product descriptions rather than only short placeholder text.

Build a useful brief for your Erode project

Start with the audience and the action you want the design to support. Then list each deliverable: size, number of pages or variants, language versions and intended use. Supply approved text and the clearest available logo or product images.

  • Language: Tell us whether you need Tamil, English or separate versions. Have a fluent reviewer approve spelling, names and technical wording.
  • Production: Send your printer’s template or dieline when relevant. Ask for a realistic size proof before approving artwork.
  • Budget: Compare the same scope, revision rounds and file formats across quotes. Additional items and a new design direction should be agreed separately.
  • Handover: Identify the editing software your team uses and the export formats your supplier needs. Editable-file delivery depends on the agreed package.

Questions from businesses planning work in Erode

Can a designer guarantee exact product color from a phone photo?

Agree on a controlled photography and proofing process with your supplier. A phone image and an ordinary screen preview are limited references for color-critical work.
