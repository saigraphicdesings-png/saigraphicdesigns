Best graphic designer in US: seven checks before you hire

A designer should be able to explain the work, show relevant examples and tell you what you will receive. Use these checks when comparing any designer, including our studio.

  1. Relevant portfolio: Ask for work similar to your project, such as labels for a packaging job or multi-page layouts for a brochure.
  2. Clear visual communication: Check typography, hierarchy, readability and consistent use of color across related designs.
  3. Production knowledge: For print, share your printer’s dimensions, bleed, safe area and export requirements.
  4. Specific scope: Confirm the number of designs, concepts, pages and adaptations.
  5. Revision process: Agree on feedback rounds and how a new direction or additional item will be quoted.
  6. Practical communication: Confirm who gives approval, the contact window and feedback deadlines for your time zone.
  7. Usable final files: Specify editable formats, exports and any asset permissions required for the intended use.

What to send for an accurate design quote

Include each deliverable and its size, number of pages or variations, budget range, final deadline, US time zone and editing software your team uses. For print projects, attach the printer’s template and production instructions.

Tell us whether you need CorelDRAW, Illustrator or Photoshop source files, print-ready PDF, SVG, PNG or JPG exports. Available formats depend on the project and software used. Revisions, turnaround and payment options are confirmed in the quote.

Is a local US designer or a remote designer right for you?

If your project requires in-person visits, on-site photography or coordination at a local print facility, a nearby provider may be a practical fit. If the work can be briefed, reviewed and delivered digitally, you can also compare remote studios by portfolio, communication, scope and file handover.
