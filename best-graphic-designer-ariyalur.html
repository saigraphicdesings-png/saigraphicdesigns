This guide uses two illustrative project briefs to show what to look for. They are planning examples, not claims about completed clients or a ranking of district designers.

Project example: A building-material supplier’s product sheet

A supplier preparing a product sheet needs product grades, pack sizes and ordering contacts to remain easy to compare. Ask the designer to organize the information into a repeatable table rather than placing every detail inside promotional graphics.

What to check in the proof

Keep technical specifications separate from sales headlines. Supply approved terminology and ask for a proof with the smallest text shown at the intended print size.

A different brief: A training center’s admission poster

Put the course, eligibility, application deadline and contact method in a clear reading order. Prepare separate print and mobile layouts so the phone number does not become too small when the poster is shared.

Build a useful brief for your Ariyalur project

Start with the audience and the action you want the design to support. Then list each deliverable: size, number of pages or variants, language versions and intended use. Supply approved text and the clearest available logo or product images.

  • Language: Tell us whether you need Tamil, English or separate versions. Have a fluent reviewer approve spelling, names and technical wording.
  • Production: Send your printer’s template or dieline when relevant. Ask for a realistic size proof before approving artwork.
  • Budget: Compare the same scope, revision rounds and file formats across quotes. Additional items and a new design direction should be agreed separately.
  • Handover: Identify the editing software your team uses and the export formats your supplier needs. Editable-file delivery depends on the agreed package.

Questions from businesses planning work in Ariyalur

Can one artwork serve as both a technical sheet and a social post?

Use the same brand elements, but request separate layouts: a technical sheet needs detailed comparisons, while a social post needs a focused message and readable contact details.
